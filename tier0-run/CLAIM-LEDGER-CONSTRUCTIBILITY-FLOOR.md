# Ledger Candidate — Constructibility Floor Before Linkage Stress

**Scope:** synthetic key-value / two-step linkage tasks under closed-world prompting.
**Current use:** organizing claim for Constructibility Preflight — Level 1.
**Not a mechanism claim · not a seam claim · not a quantization result.**
**Filed:** 2026-06-07

This entry carries **three** claims at **three** evidential classes. Keeping them separate is the point — mixing them lets a conditional borrow the authority of a definition (*the same failure the ledger exists to block*).

---

## Claim A — the principle *(analytic)*

> Linkage stress is **uninterpretable below a constructibility floor.** An INT8/INT4 composite drop cannot be attributed to linkage when the FP16 linkage baseline — contract adherence, component **and** composite correctness, operation fidelity, failure-class separability, controls, provenance — was never established.

**Class:** Method / evaluation — original framing.
**Status:** **Analytic, not empirical.** True by what "attributing a stress effect" *means*; **not falsifiable by Track A**, and it stands whether or not any cell ever clears. Sibling of *"retention does not specify what retained."*
**Short form:** linkage stress is only interpretable above a constructibility floor.

## Claim B — the mappability hypothesis *(empirical, falsifiable)*

> For closed-world two-step linkage **at 3B**, that floor is a **stable, mappable object**: pre-stress failures saturate into recurring, separable classes, and the clean/dirty distinction is interpretable.

**Class:** Open hypothesis — Track-A target; **unrun.**
**Short form:** the floor is a mappable object for this construction at 3B.

## Claim C — linkage-under-compression *(conditional; gated on B)*

> Under declared compression stress, a **verified** two-step composite may show greater behavioral vulnerability than its **verified** one-step components.

**Class:** Conditional hypothesis — **not testable until Claim B yields a cell that clears the floor.** Behavioral only.
**Safe label:** *candidate composite-specific behavioral stress vulnerability.*
**Unsafe labels:** seam found · reasoning broke · composition collapsed · mechanism identified · INT4 causes linkage failure generally.

---

## What B predicts

If B is useful, preflight cells do not fail randomly. Failures cluster into classifiable patterns (format/scaffold · wrong-chain selection · stopped-short · wrong-neighbor · non-context return · shortcut/single-hop-sufficient · positional anchoring · distractor attraction · UNCLASSIFIED/OFF-FRAME residue), and the map shows **structure**: some axes break contract first; some preserve contract but break operation fidelity; some produce separable classes; some cells become stress-eligible, others become boundary evidence.

## What supports B — and the separate Track-B unlock

**Support for B is interpretable structure, not a green cell:**
1. Dirty cells fail in recurring, classifiable ways rather than arbitrary ways.
2. Novel failure classes decrease (saturate) as cells are mapped.
3. UNCLASSIFIED/OFF-FRAME residue stays bounded and auditable.
4. Rebuild tests distinguish construction artifacts from persistent floor behavior.

> **Zero clean cells with cleanly classified dirty cells still supports B.** That is the constructibility-boundary result, not a failure.

**The Track-B unlock is a *separate* condition, not a test of B:** at least one cell clears all gates **and** survives an authorized confirmation pass. Finding a clean cell opens Track B; it does **not** "confirm the hypothesis." B is supported by structure; C is what a clean cell unlocks.

## What weakens / falsifies B

Failure classes keep expanding without saturation · UNCLASSIFIED/OFF-FRAME rises or dominates · classifier reliability fails · rebuilds produce unrelated failures every time · same-context controls cannot be stabilized · clean cells appear/disappear without a traceable axis · dirty cells cannot be classified or routed. *(These weaken **B** — the floor isn't yet a stable map. They do **not** touch **A**, which remains analytic.)*

## Safe support pattern for C *(only after a cell clears the floor)*

```
FP16:        hop1 passes · hop2 passes · composite passes ·
             operation fidelity passes · separability passes · controls pass
INT8/INT4:   components stay above gate · contract holds ·
             classes stay separable · composite degrades disproportionately
```

## What this entry forbids interpreting as linkage-under-compression evidence

A composite drop when FP16 operation fidelity was not established · when components were tested in easier context than the composite · dominated by scaffold/format failure · dominated by wrong-chain selection · where failure classes are not separable · where token construction changed without lineage tracking · after post-hoc taxonomy repair without rerun.

## Required measurement rule — canonical gate ladder

A cell is stress-eligible only after clearing, in order (a failed gate blocks those below it):

```
Gate 0    Axis-control & manifest
Gate 0.5  Token-construction audit
Gate 1    Contract adherence (contract includes NULL / NO_LINK)
Gate 2    FP16 baseline correctness — split hop1 / hop2 / composite
Gate 3    Operation fidelity (hard; positive sufficiency exclusion)
Gate 4a   Classifier reliability
Gate 4b   Failure-class separability
Gate 5    Control adequacy (incl. un-linked negative-graph control)
Gate 5.5  Baseline-stability confirmation — Manager-authorized only
Gate 6    Stress-eligibility recommendation — recommends; does not authorize stress
```

This ladder is canonical and matches *Constructibility Preflight — Level 1: Two-Stage Formal Proposal Candidate*. The two documents must not diverge.

**Stage-0 precondition.** The ladder presumes a **locked two-hop instrument** (scorer, validator, taxonomy, classifier, runner — all hashed). The current scorer is single-hop, so Gate 2/3/4b thresholds are **blocked until Stage 0 closes**: design two-hop scorer → lock hashes → verify classifier reliability → set thresholds → only then is the ladder run-ready. One-hop → two-hop is an *instrument boundary*, not an axis.

## Non-claims

A seam exists / does not exist · quantization breaks reasoning · INT4 is harmful or harmless · 3B cannot do two-hop linkage · constructibility boundary equals capability boundary · salient-token attraction is an internal mechanism · behavioral evidence implies circuit structure.

## Safe claim language

Use: *under this frozen construction, at this model size, under these gates, the task did or did not reach interpretability.*
Do not use: *the model can / cannot do two-hop linkage* · *the seam exists / does not exist* · *compression breaks composition.*

## Why this matters

Old question: *does compression break linkage?* Better current question: *where is linkage stress interpretable at all?* The constructibility-floor framing turns baseline failure from an inconvenience into data, and makes **Track A a valid research object** rather than a delay before Track B.

## One-sentence version for the team

> That you cannot read a stress effect without a clean baseline is a **definition** (Claim A); what is **open and testable** is whether that baseline floor is a stable, mappable object for this construction at 3B (Claim B) — and mapping it is the next research object, whether or not it ever yields a stress-eligible cell.
