# Status

Where each piece stands, and what's actually open — updated as the work and the literature move.

## What this repository is — and is not

This repository does **not** claim to discover new transformer mechanisms, new LLM failure modes, or a new theory of quantization. Its contribution is a **bounded behavioral metrology workflow**: measure retention under declared stress; require baseline correctness, stressed correctness, and same-error identity to be logged with every retention result, so stable *wrong behavior* cannot masquerade as robustness; and enforce a boundary between behavioral evidence and mechanistic claims through a claim-governance layer (the claim ledger and the boundary diagrams). Adjacent work already studies quantization effects on circuits and task-sensitive layer allocation; this project treats stress-retention as **behavioral evidence only**, with any mechanistic handoff gated on future data.

**On the field's mechanism, and where this sits relative to it.** The adjacent literature already supplies a (boring, in the best way) mechanistic story: compression perturbs numerical representations, high-margin decisions tend to survive, and low-margin, uncertain, or task-sensitive decisions are more likely to change — while aggregate scores can hide those item-level shifts. Prior work shows compression preserving aggregate accuracy while individual answers flip correct↔wrong; reports safety-relevant bias flips that aggregate bias scores miss, with high-uncertainty responses far more likely to change; and, from the internal side, studies precision sensitivity as uneven across circuits, layers, and tasks. So this project does **not** claim "stress reveals margin" as a discovered mechanism — that phrase is an interpretation of the field's result, not a measurement. What the project adds is strictly downstream and behavioral: when behavior is perturbed *or* stays stable under declared stress, it forces the evaluation to answer *what exactly survived* — correct capability, changed behavior, or the same wrong answer.

Stated honestly, the defensible contributions are narrow:

1. **Stress-retention protocol design** — a bounded, matched-pair design with explicit claim limits. Clean, but unrun.
2. **Correctness + same-error identity guard** — the strongest *operational* contribution: a retention-only metric scores a perfectly-retained wrong answer as strength, so baseline-correctness, stressed-correctness, and same-error identity are logged jointly to surface stable wrong *behavior* (never circuits).
3. **Boundary-first claim governance** — the strongest *process* contribution: a claim ledger plus boundary diagrams that block mechanism claims from behavioral measurement. Distinctive as *packaging*; the underlying principle overlaps with construct validity, preregistration, and model-card discipline.

Not contributions, recorded honestly as such: the analogy→parameter lens (a parked thinking tool, `notes/open-question-uneven-support.md`) and the behavioral→MI handoff (a future idea, gated on a Tier 0 signal). Neither has run; neither is claimed.

## The real problem this serves

It is worth stating plainly *why* the above matters — and being precise that this is a **measurement-validity** question, not a mechanistic one. The right "why" is not *why did the model fail internally?* (that lives in Mechanism, and the method cannot answer it). The right "why" is: *why does stress-retention evaluation fail to distinguish useful robustness from stable wrongness?* That lives in Method ∩ Evaluation, and it is the project's actual center.

> The real problem is not that models fail under stress — the field already studies that. It is that **evaluation can misclassify what survived.** A behavior that survives stress may be correct capability, a stable shortcut, fluent surface form, or a repeated error. A plain retention number blurs these together. Without correctness and same-error-identity logging, retention can certify the wrong thing — a model that is merely *robust at being wrong* scores as robust.

The cleanest statement of the center: **retention is not a single thing unless you identify what retained.** "Retained" is ambiguous — retained accuracy? output form? the same answer? the same *wrong* answer? a reasoning path? fluency? The protocol's contribution is to force that ambiguity into columns instead of letting it hide inside an aggregate score. And the boundary still holds at the end of the chain: once stable wrongness is visible, the method can say *the behavior survived incorrectly* — it cannot say *which circuit caused it.* Measurement-validity "why" is the project; mechanistic "why" stays blocked.

**What Tier 0 can and cannot settle — the scope of a result.** The run tests one thing: *does item-level correctness/error-identity reporting reveal structure that aggregate retention would hide?* The outcomes are bounded accordingly, in both directions:
- A **positive** result (high same-error retention in shortcut-prone items, larger ΔR in low-support compositional items) demonstrates the reporting rule has *interpretive value in that run* — i.e. for this model, task set, and stress configuration, aggregate retention would have hidden meaningful item-level structure. It does **not** prove the measurement matters universally.
- A **null** result (neither robust-wrong nor a ΔR gap appears) shows only that *this run did not surface the failure mode.* It does **not** retire the correctness/same-error requirement, because that requirement's justification is analytic, not empirical: "retention does not specify what retained" stays true whether or not a given run exhibits stable wrongness. A null says "under this tested setup, stable wrong retention was not observed" — nothing larger.

So the result is a **reporting-value claim, not a mechanism claim**, and it is local to its run. The honest one-line compression: *this project does not discover new failure modes; it builds a measurement contract that makes known failure modes harder to hide inside aggregate robustness claims* — the place where the dashboard can lie, and a better gauge for it.

## The open question

After several literature searches, the *explanatory* questions are answered or substantially so:

| Topic | Status |
|---|---|
| Fragility (capabilities differ in robustness under coarsening) | Supported by literature |
| Provenance effects (training composition shapes capability + robustness) | Supported by controlled studies |
| Capacity vs. usable structure | Hypothesis |
| Qualification metric (retention vs. accuracy as reliability predictor) | Open question |

Both fragility and provenance-effects have substantial prior art (see Literature Notes) — so the framework's role toward them is **synthesis**, not discovery, and the literature confirms those *phenomena* without confirming the framework's geometric *account* of them.

What remains genuinely open is *practical*, not explanatory, and sits to the side of the whole explanatory program:

> **Does a capability's retention under stress (compression, perturbation) predict its real-world deployment reliability better than its peak benchmark accuracy?**

The *concept* of reliability-style evaluation is itself active (robustness benchmarks, stress-testing, "accuracy-alone is insufficient for deployment" work). What appears possibly open is the narrow competing-predictors test: retention-under-compression vs. peak accuracy, head to head, as predictors of reliability. Its value does **not** depend on originality or on any mechanism being correct — a stress test that predicts field failure is useful regardless. This is the framework's honest forward edge.

## What would move this forward

The June 14 PIVOT updated the priority. The qualification test below remains the program's honest forward edge, but the near-term center of gravity has shifted from *running the seam on a certified baseline* (which the D4 attempt closed as a route) to *building the instrument that does the validity-gating before any retention claim can be made on any candidate.* Both directions are open; the instrument now precedes the seam.

1. **G6 standing rejection-audit build (the next model-free target).** Build and exercise the standing rejection audit specified in `tier-1-instrument/specs/g6-standing-rejection-audit-spec-v0.1.md`: trigger on every refusal; mechanized independence (blind second reader, pre-registered output-classification schema applied without route knowledge, or external ground-truth labels — at least one); five output classes (`REFUSAL-CONFIRMED` / `REFUSAL-REVERSED` / `REFUSAL-QUARANTINED` / `AUDIT-INCONCLUSIVE` / `AUDIT-CIRCULARITY`); the per-item construct-validity judgement stays human. Building and exercising it is what would raise Paper A's non-vacuousness claim from *suggested by two worked episodes* to *demonstrated by a standing mechanism.* Model-free; separately authorized.
2. **External demonstration of the gate.** Apply Paper A's gate to a baseline the program did not itself construct (the non-vacuousness bound in §6.2 of Paper A is that, to date, the gate has only refused baselines its own authors built). Independent demonstration would widen that bound. Model-free preparatory work, model-facing execution gated.
3. **Qualification test (still the field's honest forward edge).** Across a panel of models, compare *retention under stress* against *peak benchmark accuracy* as competing predictors of deployment reliability. Needs no fine-tuning, no provenance isolation, no mechanism — only a measurement that works. Possibly still open as a clean head-to-head; valuable regardless of originality. Requires a candidate that *clears* the validity gate first (D4 did not); held until a certified baseline exists.

The provenance comparison that earlier looked like the frontier has substantial controlled prior art (composition→capability, composition→perturbation-robustness) and is no longer the priority; a quantization-specific corner may remain but is likely an obvious extension others are pursuing.
