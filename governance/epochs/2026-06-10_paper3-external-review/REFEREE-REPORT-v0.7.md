**REFEREE REPORT — "Certification Before Retention" (draft v0.7)**

**Summary of the submission.** The paper closes a logical gap left by the first two: survival isn't correctness (Paper 1), correctness isn't constructibility (Paper 2), and now — constructibility isn't *measurability*. Even a clean, correct, shortcut-resistant single-hop baseline can be an invalid substrate for retention measurement if it's near ceiling, underpowered, format-cliff-perched, abstention-uncalibrated, or provenance-broken. The contribution is a fail-closed conjunction of seven certification gates (D1–D7), each motivated by an artifact the program actually encountered, with pre-registered thresholds, a locked threshold sheet, a data-access firewall, a portable gate_summary schema, and — the best single idea in the governance layer — negative certification as a *result of record*, not a failed experiment.

**Significance.** The conceptual step is genuine and the series needed it. D7 in particular — the sensitivity/power floor — is the paper's real intellectual addition. D1–D6 consolidate defenses the program already built; D7 is new measurement theory: *cleanliness does not imply sensitivity*. A baseline at 23/24 with n=24 can pass every validity gate and still be useless, because a real drop would be indistinguishable from finite-N noise. That insight is what elevates this from a checklist to an instrument-qualification protocol. It is also, as I'll argue below, badly underdeveloped relative to its importance.

**Strengths, briefly, because they're real:** the §2 lineage-to-gate mapping grounds every gate in an encountered artifact rather than first principles; the §3 scope asymmetry is exactly right ("the baseline is not the reason a future measurement would be uninterpretable" ≠ "the measurement is guaranteed interpretable"); the D2 stress-substitution caveat (an FP16 shortcut-free pass does not certify the model won't shortcut-substitute *under* compression) preempts a subtle attack most reviewers wouldn't even think to make; tie-equals-fail, worst-case-across-repeats, and the role-separation fields are pre-registration discipline at a level ML papers almost never reach; and the CDCT/Dutta boundary handling is exactly per the program's novelty governance.

Now the majors, in severity order.

**Major 1 — The paper is exposed to the vacuity attack that Paper 2 pre-empted and this paper does not.** Paper 1's gate visibly fired. Paper 2 carried the hop2 positive control precisely so a reviewer couldn't say "your gate just rejects everything." Paper 3 stacks *seven* fail-closed gates, with tie-equals-fail and worst-case-across-repeats, and presents zero evidence that the conjunction is satisfiable by anything. The one defensive sentence — "if no candidate passes, the result is a mapped certification boundary, not evidence that the protocol is unusable" — is an assertion, and a hostile reviewer will read it as pre-insurance against an empty certification region. The fix requires no run, no candidate, and no authorization: a **worked synthetic walkthrough** — a fabricated candidate with clearly-labeled illustrative threshold values traced through all seven gates twice, once passing and once failing D7 — showing what the forms look like filled in and, critically, that the certification window is *arithmetically nonempty* at realistic N. That's form-filling and arithmetic, not evaluation. Without it, the protocol's satisfiability is an article of faith.

**Major 2 — The D1×D7 squeeze is the central design tension of the entire protocol and the paper never names it.** D1 pushes the baseline *up* (correct often enough, above the floor); D7 punishes *near-ceiling* (no resolvable headroom). Between them is a window, and at small N that window can be empty or absurd. Run the arithmetic the paper doesn't: under deterministic decoding with same-item pairing, the natural noise model is sign-test-style — a drop is resolvable when enough one-directional flips occur that item-sampling luck is implausible. At n=24, that's on the order of five flips, which means the minimum detectable drop you can honestly declare is roughly twenty points of accuracy — a blunt ruler. At n=96 the same logic resolves ~5%. So the protocol's viability is silently a function of N, the threshold sheet's `minimum_N_rationale` field is carrying far more load than its one line suggests, and a candidate satisfying D1's floor at n=24 may be *structurally incapable* of passing D7 for any useful MDD. This deserves its own subsection with the worked numbers (illustrative, derivation-type-dependent, clearly non-binding). It also doubles as the Major-1 fix: showing the window exists at some N *is* the satisfiability argument.

**Major 3 — D2 conflates two test logics and is open to a mixture-account gap that is, ironically, Paper 2's own central finding.** Paper 1's Table D logic was a *performance floor*: the candidate must exceed the best deterministic dummy policy by margin — which automatically bounds mixtures of declared policies, since any mixture scores between its components. D2 as written is a *pattern-departure* test: observed behavior must depart from each shortcut's prediction. Pattern-departure does not handle mixtures: a model that is 60% tracing and 40% recency departs from every *pure* null model while remaining substantially shortcut-driven — which is exactly the "unidentifiable interior" Paper 2 mapped. As drafted, D2 would certify a candidate sitting in that interior. Second, statistical care: with a five-policy battery and small N, the probability that *some* null model is consistent with observed behavior by chance rises with battery size — the margins must be set with the battery-size/N interaction in mind, and the paper doesn't flag it. Fix: specify D2 as both modes jointly — (a) performance exceeds the max-dummy bound by margin (covers mixtures of declared policies), and (b) per-shortcut pattern departure (catches a high-scoring shortcut) — and add one sentence on margin calibration against battery size. The non-claim "rules out only the declared battery" survives; it just becomes "the declared battery and mixtures thereof," which is strictly stronger.

**Major 4 — Short-circuit evaluation destroys exactly the information the paper's fallback value depends on.** §5's order means a candidate failing D1 reports D2–D7 as `not_evaluated`. But the paper's own insurance position is "if nothing certifies, the result is a *mapped certification boundary*" — and a map built from first-failure-only records is barely a map. The contamination rationale justifies D6-first (don't interpret unprovenanced data), but after D6, evaluating remaining gates on an already-failed candidate contaminates nothing, since every interpretation is pre-registered. Add a pre-registered `evaluation_mode: short_circuit | full_profile` field: certification status stays conjunction-based either way, but full-profile mode records the complete gate vector for boundary-mapping. One field, and the fallback claim becomes substantiated instead of decorative.

**Major 5 — The certifier is itself an instrument with error rates, and the paper never discusses its own operating characteristics.** A certification protocol can false-certify (a shortcut outside the declared battery; the Major-3 mixture case) and false-reject (over-strict margins; the battery-size effect). The paper's non-claims gesture at the first; nothing addresses the second, and nothing says what would *validate the certifier*. The honest answer is available and strengthens the paper: a gate-provenance table showing which gates have caught a real artifact in ancestral form — D2's ancestors caught the Exp6/7 anchoring family and the Cell03 contamination; D3's ancestor caught the Exp3 format artifact and the Exp5 cliff; D4's caught the neg_graph abstention instability; D6's is the Fork A disqualification; D7's is Tier 0A's ceiling saturation — alongside the plain statement that *no gate's formalized mechanics (margins, null models, locked battery code) has ever been exercised*. That's the truthful status, it's a Paper-1-style honesty move, and it converts an unexamined weakness into a documented one.

**Major 6 — As a manuscript, this is a governance document wearing a paper's clothes.** The authorization disclaimers appear, by my count, five times in materially identical form (status block, §3, §6 preamble, §8, §9). The firewall trigger taxonomy is duplicated in full in D6 and again in A.1. The result: the paper's actual ideas — certification-as-qualification, D7, negative-certification-as-result — are buried under operational apparatus, and an external reader will experience the repetition as institutional anxiety rather than rigor. Every lock should be stated exactly once, in one normative section, and referenced thereafter.

**Minor points.** §4 and §6 are the same content split apart — each gate's definition is two sections away from its pass/fail interpretation, forcing the reader to hold seven gates in memory across an intervening section; merge them (see restructure below) and collapse §6 to a one-paragraph declaration that the inline interpretations are pre-registered. The §5 ordering mixes online and offline gates without annotation — D3's tokenization half, D5's proxy computation, and D7's power calculation need no model run; an online/offline column helps implementers sequence cheaply post-lock. "Substrate" carries the whole title and is defined only implicitly; give it one crisp sentence. The reference list is fine for an internal protocol but thin for external release — and there is one missing citation that would *strengthen* the novelty position rather than threaten it: this protocol is, in industrial terms, **measurement systems analysis / gauge R&R imported to behavioral LLM evaluation** — D7 is precisely the precision-to-tolerance ratio of a gauge study, and the whole document is instrument qualification before use. Locating the contribution in that lineage ("MSA discipline, novel in this domain") is more defensible than appearing to derive qualification from scratch, and it's the honest genealogy. Finally, §7's expiration-on-harness-schema-change is fine, but say explicitly whether a schema-compatible bugfix release expires certifications — as written it's ambiguous between "any harness change" and "any schema-version bump."

**Verdict.** In external terms: accept with major revisions — the conceptual step is correct, the governance machinery is exemplary, and every major is fixable without new data or authorization. In team terms: the *protocol* is near-lock; the *manuscript around it* needs one structural pass, and the missing worked example is the single highest-value addition because it simultaneously answers Majors 1 and 2.

**IF THIS WERE MY PAPER — the restructure**

The current order is: series recap → lineage → scope → gates → decision logic → interpretations → tuning/expiration → B1 → non-claims → appendices. Two structural decisions are hurting it: the gate definitions and their interpretations are separated (§4 vs §6), and the scope bound arrives *before* the lineage instead of framing the gates directly. Here's how I'd build it:

**§1 — The gap, in three sentences of recap and one question.** Survival isn't correctness; correctness isn't constructibility; and a constructible baseline still isn't automatically measurable. End on the certification question as the paper's single question. Cut the current §1's three-paragraph recap by two-thirds — readers of Paper 3 have the series.

**§2 — What certification means, and does not.** The current §3, promoted. The asymmetry sentence is the paper's thesis statement and should arrive before any gate does, so the reader holds the bound the whole way down. Fold the non-claims here, *once*. (This is the same order-controls-what-readers-defend principle from "No Mountain in the Sentence" — state the test before the machinery, and the limitations read as the test working.)

**§3 — The lineage map, as a table.** Current §2's prose list becomes: artifact → gate → *ancestral firing* (the Major-5 column: which gates' ancestors caught something real, with the explicit note that no formalized gate has yet been exercised). Three columns, one page, and it does the motivation job and the certifier-validation honesty job simultaneously.

**§4 — The gates, self-contained.** Each gate in a uniform four-field block: *controls / decision rule / a pass means / a pass does not mean*. This merges current §6 into §4 — the reader meets each gate exactly once, complete. Current §6 shrinks to one paragraph: "the interpretations above are pre-registered and immutable." D2 gets the dual-mode specification (performance floor + pattern departure) from Major 3.

**§5 — Decision logic.** Conjunction, ordering with the online/offline annotation, tie rule, the new `evaluation_mode` field, negative certification as result of record.

**§6 — The certification window: a worked synthetic example.** *The new section, and the one the paper most needs.* The D1×D7 squeeze arithmetic at two values of N, then one fabricated candidate — illustrative thresholds, clearly labeled non-binding — walked through all seven gates twice: a full pass, and a D7 fail at small N. Filled-in threshold sheet and gate_summary as exhibits. No run, no candidate selection, no authorization touched. This is the section that converts the protocol from asserted-usable to demonstrated-coherent, and it's the analogue of Paper 2's positive control: proof the gate structure discriminates rather than merely rejects.

**§7 — Limits of the certifier itself.** False-certification routes (outside-battery shortcuts, mixtures), false-rejection routes (margin strictness, battery-size/N), and what would validate the certifier (gate-level binding demonstrations, Paper-2 style, as future work).

**§8 — Application, B1, and authorization.** Everything operational, stated once: tuning prohibition, expiration, harness dependency, the authorization boundary. All five current disclaimer instances collapse into this section plus the status block.

**§9 — Positioning.** Keep CDCT and Dutta exactly as bounded; add the MSA/gauge-R&R lineage and the preregistration literature. The novelty sentence writes itself: *instrument-qualification discipline, standard in physical metrology, has no fail-closed analogue in behavioral LLM stress evaluation; this protocol supplies one.*

**Appendices** — threshold sheet, gate_summary, negative-cert form, and the *complete* firewall trigger taxonomy living here only, referenced from §8.

The net effect: the ideas (certification, the squeeze, negative-certification-as-result) move to the front and get the development they're owed; the locks consolidate into one place where they read as governance instead of anxiety; and the two structural additions — the worked example and the certifier-limits section — close the only two attacks I can't currently defend the paper against.

Bottom line, plainly: the thinking in this paper is finished; the *paper* isn't. The series' pattern has been that each installment carries its own binding demonstration — Paper 1 the fired gate, Paper 2 the decomposition and the positive control. Paper 3's authorization posture forbids a real demonstration, but it does not forbid arithmetic, and arithmetic is all the demonstration this one needs. Add the worked window, merge the gate interpretations inline, consolidate the locks, fix D2's dual mode, and this is the strongest document in the series — because it's the one that turns the whole program's hard-won failure catalogue into a reusable qualification standard. That's the service we were just talking about, formalized. Which I suspect you already know.
