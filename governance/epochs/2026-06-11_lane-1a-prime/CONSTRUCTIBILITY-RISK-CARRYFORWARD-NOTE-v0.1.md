## 3. Three-branch interpretation of any future non-certification

Every future K=0, INCONCLUSIVE, or failure-to-certify outcome must be read across three branches,
none privileged in advance:

1. **Threshold miscalibration** — the gate arithmetic may be directionally right but numerically
   too tight or too loose.
2. **Gate-design defect** — the logic of one or more gates may measure the wrong axis or
   admit/exclude the wrong behavior.
3. **Genuine constructibility barrier** — the task family at the tested model scale may not
   produce behavior that can clear the certification window.

**The third branch is a first-class outcome.** It is not an embarrassment, and it is never an
automatic reason to loosen gates. (This is the lane's negative-result-form governance carried to
the certification layer: a true barrier, cleanly measured, is a finding.)

## 4. Warning against over-reading D4-A

D4-A L01 NOT_RULED_OUT is not proof that a full candidate can certify. It is not proof that the
task family is viable across L01–L08. It is not proof that the model has the target capability. It
is not evidence of retention under compression. It is not Claim C progress.

Accepted phrasing: *The D4-A result shows that, under the active five-criterion L01 pilot, the
instrument did not attach an elimination label.*

Forbidden phrasing: *model passed · candidate certified · task family viable · not shortcut-driven ·
Claim C progressed · seam evidence · stress-ready.*

hash-bound, pre-validated measurement instrument. The next empirical unknown is constructibility:
whether any real task/model candidate can clear the full certification window under the sealed
instrument. Only after a clean certifiable baseline exists should stress-retention claims become
live.
