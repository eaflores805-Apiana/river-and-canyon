## 0. Scope

Names the *classes* of prerequisite that must hold before any quantization-stress retention reading can be interpreted. It enumerates kinds, not quantities. It sets no thresholds, names no candidates, selects no stress level, and authorizes nothing. It is the qualitative companion to Block F (which owns all feasibility math) and to Paper 3's D0 posture (certification before retention).

## 1. Prerequisite classes (named only)

**A. Substrate-certification prerequisites.** Before a surface can host a retention reading, it must have cleared the certification gate as a structure — not as a score. The classes:
- a constructible clean baseline whose correctness is not produced by a shortcut (the Paper 2 constructibility class);
- a certified substrate per the D1–D7 conjunction (the Paper 3 substrate class);
- a non-saturated operating point — the surface must sit off its accuracy ceiling with resolvable room for a drop, or no downward measurement is interpretable (the D7 saturation class; this is the class Block F found binding for the D4 family).

**B. Instrument-sensitivity prerequisites.** Before a *drop* can be attributed to compression rather than to a blind instrument:
- demonstrated criterion-firing under controlled conditions (the Layer-1 class);
- demonstrated real-candidate elimination capability in the relevant condition class, or an explicit, recorded statement of its absence (the Layer-2 class);
- a control channel that reads native emission bias rather than capability (the token-prior / control-channel class).

**C. Artifact/concept-correspondence prerequisites.** Before any stress artifact is trusted to mean what its name claims:
- shown semantic-read of every load-bearing stress artifact — the stress schedule, the rung specification, the comparison schema — under the adopted gate (the semantic-read class; this is the class Path A exposed);
- classification of each stress artifact as instrument-component vs configuration before sealing (the classification-audit class);
- surplus-semantics consideration for stress artifacts — that a stress condition instantiates the intended compression and no uncontrolled second concept (the surplus class; provisional).

**D. Identity- and error-tracking prerequisites.** Before a drop is read as the *same* capability degrading rather than a different error appearing:
- same-item correspondence across the unstressed and stressed conditions (the item-identity class);
- same-error-identity tracking — distinguishing "the model now fails the items it used to pass" from "the model fails different items at a similar rate" (the error-identity class);
- provenance binding of stressed artifacts to their unstressed origins (the lineage class).

## 2. Failure classes that block stress interpretation (named only)

A retention reading is uninterpretable, regardless of the number it produces, if any of these is present:
- saturation masking — the baseline scores at ceiling, so a drop cannot be resolved from noise (the D4 family's current condition per Block F);
- shortcut-preserved survival — the stressed surface "survives" via a shortcut the unstressed surface also used, so retention measures preserved error (the Paper 1 class);
- blind-instrument ambiguity — no demonstrated elimination capability, so a non-drop cannot be distinguished from an insensitive battery (the Layer-2-absent class);
- artifact/concept drift — a stress artifact does not instantiate the compression it is named for (the Path A class, one layer down);
- error-identity confound — equal aggregate accuracy masking a changed error set (the error-identity class).

## 3. D0 implication (certification before retention)

These classes are why D0 sits before the retention rungs and not after: each is a precondition whose failure makes a retention number a measurement of something other than retention. Block G records the classes; it does not order, weight, or quantify them — that is downstream certification work, gated and unauthorized here. The single most operationally relevant class today is the non-saturation prerequisite (A), because Block F has already found it binding for the only surface family in hand.
