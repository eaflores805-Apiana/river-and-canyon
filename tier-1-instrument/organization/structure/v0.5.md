## §A. The principle: express the distinction in structure; do not rename to flatten

When an artifact's true category does not match its naive directory-listing appearance, the correct response is to **make the real distinction visible in the structure** — a directory, a sub-tree, a path that says what the thing is — **not to rename or relocate the artifact so the listing looks tidier at the cost of the distinction.** Renaming-to-flatten trades a true category for a convenient appearance; it is a loss disguised as cleanup.

This principle is recorded here because it has now governed three independent cases the same way:

```text
CASE 1 — Paper A vs the numbered series.
  Paper A ("before-retention") is the INSTRUMENT paper, first of the lettered
  instrument/stress dyad (A, with future B), categorically distinct from the
  numbered metrology series (1, 2, 3). A brief rename of its directory to
  "paper4-" flattened that distinction (and by symmetry implied a wrong "paper5-"
  for Paper B). RESOLUTION: reverted to paper-a-before-retention/; the letter
  marks the dyad, which the structure now shows. (Express, don't flatten.)

CASE 2 — the structure-spec / verification sub-folders.
  revisions/, verifications/, organization/ are not standalone top-level tracks;
  they are sub-structure that belongs WITH the artifact they revise or verify.
  RESOLUTION (v0.4 §6): routed with-parent — a verification belongs to the thing
  it verifies — rather than hoisted into their own tracks. (Express the real
  relationship in placement.)

CASE 3 — Hash Integrity vs Paper 4.
  The Hash Integrity standing note is versioned, has a PDF and figures, and looks
  like a paper — but it was deliberately designated a STANDING GOVERNANCE NOTE,
  NOT Paper 4: the third methodological discipline companioning the numbered
  series (survival≠correctness; correctness≠constructibility; hash-integrity≠
  construct-validity), not a member of the numbered or lettered paper series.
  RESOLUTION: a papers/standing-notes/ sub-tree, so it is discoverable from
  papers/ (closing the listing-surprise) while the sub-dir name preserves the
  "not Paper 4" designation. (Express the distinction; do not promote to flatten.)
```

The shape is identical across all three: an artifact whose category surprises a reader scanning `ls` is resolved by *structure that states the category*, never by a rename that erases it. The standing rule: **when the listing surprises, express the real distinction in structure; never rename to flatten.**

## §B. Why Hash Integrity belongs under standing-notes, not the numbered sequence

The Hash Integrity note distills a methodological discipline — *hashes bind bytes; they do not bind concepts* (a path/hash-valid artifact can fail to instantiate the concept it is named for) — and was Manager-designated a standing governance note, explicitly **not Paper 4** and not a model-behavior paper. It is the artifact/concept-layer companion to the numbered tower's claim-layer disciplines, not a rung in that tower.

Placing it in the numbered sequence (as "Paper 4") would assert it is the fourth metrology paper, which it is not; leaving it only in `governance/standing/` left it undiscoverable from `papers/` for a reader who reasonably expects paper-class artifacts there. The `papers/standing-notes/` sub-tree resolves both: paper-class governance artifacts that are deliberately not released as numbered or lettered papers live there, discoverable from `papers/`, visibly distinct from the series. (Filed under Manager Option 3; CS-verified byte-identical at `fd9f653`.)

## §C. Why Paper A remains distinct from the numbered metrology series

Paper A is the **instrument paper** — it builds and justifies the fail-closed validity gate that gates retention claims at the baseline. It is the first of a **lettered instrument/stress dyad**: Paper A (the instrument) and a future Paper B (the certified-baseline → compression-stress → retention experiment that the gate makes interpretable). The numbered series (1, 2, 3) is the metrology tower of claim-layer disciplines that builds toward the seam.

The letter is not a missing number; it encodes that this paper is a *different kind* — paired with B, not sequenced after 3. Renumbering Paper A to "4" would fold a deliberately-distinct lineage into the series it stands apart from, and would mis-imply Paper B as "5" (the fifth metrology paper rather than the stress half of the dyad). The `paper-b/planning/` placeholder directory is the structural proof the dyad is intentional: its emptiness reserves the lettered slot. The structure therefore shows two axes — a numbered tower and a lettered dyad — and must not collapse them into one.

## §D. Why repo structure should preserve artifact status, not hide it through naming

A repository's directory structure is itself a claim about what each artifact *is* — released paper vs working draft, official vs quarantined, instrument vs tower, governance note vs publication. When naming is chosen for listing-tidiness over truth, the structure makes a false claim about status, and a future reader (or a future maintainer mid-move) inherits the false claim. The program's broader discipline — *do not let a convenient appearance substitute for the verified thing* — applies to the file tree exactly as it applies to a retention score. Structure should make artifact status legible at a glance; where a true distinction is inconvenient to display, the answer is to display it clearly, not to rename until it disappears. This is the general statement of which §A is the operational rule.

## §E. The root-required-doc rule (surfaced by the repo-move closeout)

The repo move initially relocated `README.md`, `STATUS.md`, and `REVIEW.md` into `_meta/` alongside other repo-level docs, then restored them to the workspace root (closeout §15, commit `e97c7ad`). The closeout candidly recorded this as a v0.4 move-plan defect: v0.4 flagged `.gitignore` as root-required (git semantics) but did not flag that README/STATUS/REVIEW are **root-required for GitHub-entry-point semantics** — GitHub renders the root `README.md` as the repository's front page, and root-facing status/review docs are expected there.

**Rule (recorded for future moves):** the following remain at the workspace root and are not relocated into `_meta/` or any sub-tree:

```text
- .gitignore                          (git semantics)
- README.md                           (GitHub entry point; rendered as repo front page)
- STATUS.md, REVIEW.md                (root-facing program status/review)
Any future move plan must flag root-required docs explicitly, the same way
.gitignore was flagged, before relocating repo-level documents.
```

`_meta/` holds repo-level pointers and onboarding (e.g. the top-level INDEX pointer, ONBOARDING-CS), not the root-required front-page docs.

## §F. The forward-reference-note pattern for frozen documents; structure-clarification vs claim-change

**Forward-reference-note pattern.** Prior structure-spec and inventory versions are frozen artifacts (supersede-don't-rewrite): a v0.4 spec describes the structure as it stood at v0.4, and editing it to match a later relocation would rewrite history. When a later move changes a path that a frozen, still-authoritative document references, the path drift is resolved **not by editing the frozen document** but by filing a dated **forward-reference note** that records the old→new path mapping and instructs readers to resolve the old path to the new one on disk. The Hash Integrity relocation used exactly this: `HASH-INTEGRITY-RELOCATION-NOTE-2026-06-14.md` resolves the path for the two currently-authoritative pre-relocation documents (the v0.4 structure spec and the v0.2 inventory), which remain unedited. A future spec version may supersede such a note by reflecting the path directly; until then, the note is the resolver.

**Structure-clarification vs claim-change (the load-bearing line).** Everything in v0.5 — and in the three §A cases, the relocations, the root-doc restoration, the forward-reference notes — is **structure clarification: where an artifact lives and what its category is.** None of it is **claim change: what an artifact asserts about the model, the task, or the world.** The two are kept strictly separate:

```text
STRUCTURE CLARIFICATION (what v0.5 does):       CLAIM CHANGE (what v0.5 must NOT do):
  - move an artifact to a truer path               - alter what a paper or note asserts
  - name a category so it is discoverable          - promote a note to a paper (or vice versa)
  - record a placement principle                   - renumber/relabel to imply a new status
  - resolve path drift via forward-reference       - create, strengthen, or scope a claim
A relocation or a category name never changes the scientific meaning of the
artifact relocated. Hash Integrity asserts exactly what it asserted before Option
3; Paper A asserts exactly what it asserted before the rename and revert. The file
tree was clarified; not one claim moved.
```
